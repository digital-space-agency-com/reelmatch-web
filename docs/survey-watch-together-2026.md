# Survey: how people choose what to watch together

_Drafted 28 September 2026. Goal: original, quotable numbers for a press-ready
report page and pitches (the app's own match data is too small to publish yet:
322 matches across 23 friendships). Nothing here mentions ReelMatch until the
optional last question, so the headline findings aren't led._

## Setup

| Setting | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Panel | Pollfish or SurveyMonkey Audience |
| Audience | US adults 18+, balanced to census on age, gender and region (panel default) |
| Sample | 500 completes after the screener (margin of error about ±4.4 points at 95% confidence) |
| Cost | Roughly $1–3 per complete depending on panel and targeting; check current pricing |
| Length | 12 questions, about 3 minutes |
| Later | A Mexico version in Spanish (300–500 completes) for Spanish-language press |

Question order matters: keep Q1–Q10 before the optional product question (Q11).
Randomize answer order where marked **(randomize)**.

## Screener

**S1. In a typical month, how often do you watch movies or TV shows at home together with at least one other person (for example a partner, family, friends or roommates)?**
- Daily or almost daily
- Several times a week
- About once a week
- 1–3 times a month
- Less than once a month → end survey
- Never → end survey

**S2. Who do you most often watch with?** (single choice)
- My partner or spouse
- My family, including children
- Other adult family members
- Friends or roommates
- Someone else

_S2 is the main segment for the report: couples, families, friends._

## Headlines the data could support

Fill in only what the results actually show. Don't round in the direction of
the story.

- "X% of Americans who watch together spend more than 20 minutes deciding what to watch" (Q1)
- "1 in X say they gave up and watched nothing at least once in the past month" (Q3)
- "X% rewatch old favorites because choosing something new is harder" (Q4)
- "Too many options" vs "different tastes": which is the bigger obstacle (Q6)
- "Couples vs families vs friends": who takes longest to decide (Q1 by S2)
- "More subscriptions, more scrolling?" Decision time by number of services (Q1 by Q8)
- "X% say a trailer helps them decide more than a poster" (Q9)
